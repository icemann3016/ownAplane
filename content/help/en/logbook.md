# Logbook

**Menu → Logbook.** Your pilot logbook, kept in the EASA format (AMC1 FCL.050): the same columns
as a paper logbook, with totals for every column.

## What goes in

| Entry | What to enter |
|---|---|
| **Flight** | Date, departure and arrival (place and UTC time), aircraft type and registration, single-pilot time (SE/ME) or multi-pilot time, total time of flight, name of the PIC, landings (day/night), night and IFR time, pilot function time (PIC, co-pilot, dual, instructor) and remarks. |
| **Simulator session** | Date, type of device and session time. |
| **Totals brought forward** | The totals of your previous logbooks per column, as of the date of your last entry there. Your totals to date start from them. |

Times are entered as hours:minutes (`1:25`) or decimal hours (`1.4`).

## Rentals on ownAplane

When the owner confirms the flight log of your rental, each leg is added as a **draft**: route,
block times, aircraft, landings and PIC time are filled in. Complete what the app can't know
(night or IFR time, single- or multi-engine) and certify it.

## Drafts and certified entries

- An entry is a **draft** until you **certify** it. Drafts can be changed or deleted.
- **Certified entries can't be changed or deleted** — like a line in a paper logbook. To fix one,
  open it and choose **Correct this entry**: you write a new entry, and once you certify it the
  original stays in your logbook, struck through, with a link to its correction.
- An entry that shouldn't be there at all (e.g. entered twice) can be **struck through** with the
  reason. It stays visible and no longer counts.
- The app points out times that don't add up (for example PIC time more than the total), but you
  decide.

## Reading your paper logbook

**Logbook → Read a paper logbook page.** A paper EASA logbook records each flight on one line
that runs across two facing pages. Photograph or scan both pages (as two files, or one photo of
the spread) and press **Read the pages**:

- every line with a readable date becomes a **draft**, linked to the scanned page;
- the app adds up the lines and compares them with the **"total this page"** row you wrote at
  the bottom: columns that don't match are listed, so you know which page to look at again;
- lines it couldn't read are counted, so you can add them yourself.

Check the drafts against the page and certify them. The pages are sent to our AI provider
(Anthropic) only to read them. A sharp, straight photo in good light reads best; there is a daily
limit on how many pages can be read.

## Print and export

- **Print (EASA layout)** shows each page as the two facing pages of a paper logbook, with the
  total of the page, the total from previous pages and the total time. Print it or save it as PDF
  from your browser.
- **Download CSV** gives every certified and struck-through entry for a spreadsheet.

The logbook is kept so it can serve as your logbook under EASA rules; ask your authority if they
also expect a paper logbook or particular endorsements.
